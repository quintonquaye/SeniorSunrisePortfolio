# 01 — Product Brief

> **Stage 1 of 15 | Demonstrates: Product Thinking**

---

## Product Name

[What is the name of this feature or product?
Example: "Medication Manager" / "Daily Health Check-In" / "Emergency SOS System"]

---

## Problem

[Describe the real problem you are solving. Be specific.
- Who experiences this problem?
- When does it happen?
- What are the consequences if it's not solved?
- Why do existing solutions fall short?

A strong problem statement is specific and grounded in reality — not generic.
Example of weak: "Users need to manage medications."
Example of strong: "Senior users managing 5+ daily medications forget doses due to complex schedules, causing preventable health complications."]

---

## Target User

[Describe the specific person who uses this feature.
- Age range
- Technical comfort level
- Physical needs or limitations
- Context of use (at home, at a clinic, on the go?)

The more specific, the better. A senior user ≠ a professional user ≠ a caregiver.]

---

## User Need

[Write this as a one-sentence user perspective statement:
"As a [type of user], I need [what they need] because [why it matters to them]."]

---

## Proposed Solution

[Describe what you will build to solve the problem.
- What does the feature do?
- What is the core experience?
- What technology approach will you take (mobile, web, etc.)?
- What makes your approach the right one?]

---

## Product Goals

[List 3-5 specific goals this feature should achieve.
Think about: user experience goals, technical goals, business goals.
Example:
- Reduce missed doses by sending timely reminders
- Make dose logging require no more than 2 taps
- Keep all health data private and encrypted on the device]

---

## Non-Goals

[List things this feature will NOT do — and briefly say why.
This is as important as listing what it WILL do.
It shows you can make focused, deliberate scope decisions.
Example:
- ❌ Drug interaction checking — out of scope for V1
- ❌ Cloud sync — offline-first priority
- ❌ Multi-user accounts — single user only]

---

## Basic User Experience

[Describe the core user flow in plain steps. No technical details — just what the user does.
Example:
1. User opens app → sees today's medications
2. User taps "Mark as Taken"
3. Adherence updates
4. User receives a reminder notification at scheduled time]

---

## Success Criteria

[How will you know the feature is successful? Be measurable.
Example:
| Criterion | Target |
|---|---|
| Dose logging completes in under 2 taps | 100% of flows |
| Feature works offline | 100% of core features |
| All data encrypted | 100% of records |]

---

## Constraints

[What limitations must you design around?
These might be technical, user, business, or legal constraints.
Example:
- Must work offline (no internet required)
- Must be accessible for seniors (large tap targets, readable fonts)
- No backend changes allowed (client-side only)
- Must comply with PHI privacy requirements]
